01Concrete & Char

Block & Wood Shelf
With Lights

A stacked cinder-block bookshelf with hidden warm-glow lighting. Dry-stacked pine and concrete, painted matte black and stained warm walnut — it reads like architecture, not furniture, and goes together with adhesive and a drill. No joinery required.

Build Time
1 weekend
Est. Cost
$80–150
Difficulty
Beginner–Intermediate
Setting
Indoor, ~6 ft tall
Why It Works
  • No power saw required — most lumber yards will pre-cut your boards to length
  • Fully modular: build it 2 shelves short and stout, or 4 tall and dramatic
  • Doubles as display, storage, and a warm reading-nook light source
  • Inexpensive — concrete block and pine cost a fraction of a built bookcase
What You’ll Need
Material / ItemQty & Notes
Concrete (cinder) blocks, 8×8×16″8–12, depending on height (4 per column)
Pine boards (2×10 or 2×12)1 per shelf, cut to your desired length
Heavy-duty construction adhesive (masonry-rated)1–2 tubes
Exterior/masonry paint, matte black1 quart (covers 8–12 blocks)
Wood stain + sealer (warm walnut/amber)1 quart
Sandpaper, 120–150 grit1 sheet
Warm-white LED strip lights, adhesive-backedLength = total shelf-edge run
Flicker/flame-effect LED tea lights or pucks1 per block cavity you want lit
Wall anchor bracket or anti-tip strap + screws1 set, matched to your wall type
Drill / driver Stud finder Level Tape measure Paintbrushes Safety glasses Work gloves Dust mask
Safety — Read Before Starting
Anchor it — this is not optionalThe blocks are dry-stacked, not mortared. Run adhesive between every course and anchor the top course to a wall stud before loading it with books. A tall, top-heavy stack of loose block is a real tip-over risk around kids and pets.
Lift smartEach block runs 25–35 lb. Lift with your legs, and get a second person for the top courses once the unit is above shoulder height.
Ventilate while finishingPaint and stain fumes build up fast indoors — open windows and run a fan, and let everything off-gas before bringing it into a closed bedroom.
LightingUse battery or low-voltage LED only, inside the block cavities and under the shelves — never run mains-voltage wiring into an enclosed masonry space.
Step-by-Step
1
Plan your layout: decide block courses (height) and column spacing (width), and locate wall studs for the anchor point.
2
Dry-fit first — stand one block at each end, lay a shelf board across them, and confirm spacing and level before adhering anything.
3
Build up one course at a time: adhesive on top of each block, set the next block, then bridge with a shelf board. Repeat to your desired height.
4
Anchor the top course to a wall stud with your bracket or anti-tip strap.
5
Sand any rough wood edges smooth.
6
Paint the blocks: two coats of matte black masonry paint, letting each coat dry fully.
7
Stain the shelves, wipe excess, and let cure per the product’s instructions — often 24 hours before loading weight.
8
Install lighting: adhesive LED strip along the front underside edge of each shelf, flicker LEDs tucked into the cavities you want lit.
9
Style it: layer in books, plants, and a few framed pieces, varying height and grouping in odd numbers.
Styling & Finish Options
  • Group books by color or height for a curated look
  • Mix in 1–2 plants for texture against the matte black block
  • Leave at least one cavity per column empty/lit — it reads as intentional, not crowded
  • A single oversized item on the top shelf anchors the whole piece
Field Notes
  • Buy 1–2 extra blocks in case one chips during handling
  • Have your lumber yard pre-cut boards to length — saves time and mess at home
  • Test LED placement and brightness before committing to the adhesive backing; it’s a one-shot stick
  • A thin shim under one column is the fastest fix for an uneven floor

  • Pairs well with a low-profile sofa so the infinity effect is visible from seated height
  • Works equally well as a side table if you want a smaller version
Field Notes
  • Ask tire shops for ‘scrap’ tires — cosmetically good tires that failed inspection for tread depth are usually free
  • If tempered glass isn’t affordable locally, a thick acrylic disc is a lighter, safer substitute
  • Test the LED ring’s brightness and placement before sealing the glass — it’s difficult to adjust afterward

03Cast Glass

Stained-Glass
Mosaic Walkway

Hand-poured concrete steppingstones, each one inlaid with a mosaic of colored glass and a hidden solar light. By day, a garden path with real craftsmanship underfoot. After dark, it glows from inside the stone.

Build Time
1 weekend (pavers) + 1 day (install)

$150–300+

Intermediate–Advanced

Outdoor garden path
Why It Works
  • Each paver is a one-of-a-kind handmade piece — no two patterns are the same
  • A great way to reuse stained-glass or craft-glass scraps
  • The solar-lit glow effect makes the path genuinely magical at night
  • Scales to any path length — make 4 pavers or 40
What You’ll Need
Material / ItemQty & Notes
Reusable paver mold (wood frame + corner brackets)1, sized to your paver (e.g. 12×12″)
Quick-setting concrete mix1 bag per 2–3 pavers (verify with your mold volume)
Mixing bucket + paddle/drill attachment, trowel1 set
Colored glass mosaic tiles (smooth-edged/tumbled preferred)Enough to cover each paver face
Solar puck lights, waterproof1 per paver
Orbital sander or angle grinder with polishing pad1
Landscape / weed barrier fabricLength of your path
White river rock or pea gravelBy the cubic foot, sized to your path
Paver / brick edging + spikesLength of your path × 2 sides
Spade / edging shovel Wheelbarrow Level Work gloves Eye protection Rubber mallet Dust mask
Safety — Read Before Starting
Glass handlingWear gloves and eye protection while placing glass pieces — even tumbled glass can have sharp edges before it’s set in concrete.
Grinding dustSanding/grinding cured concrete creates fine dust that’s harmful to breathe. Wear an N95 mask and work outdoors or with strong ventilation.
Cure time mattersLet each paver cure per your concrete product’s instructions — typically 24–48 hours before grinding, and about 7 days before regular foot traffic.
Slip riskSmooth glass-topped pavers can be slick when wet. Consider a non-slip sealer, or mix a few glass-feature pavers in with plain textured ones.
Step-by-Step
1
Design your layout: decide paver count and size, and sketch your mosaic pattern (or keep it freeform).
2
Set up your mold on a flat, level surface.
3
Mix concrete to a thick, scoopable consistency and pour it about halfway into the mold.
4
Press a solar puck light face-up into the wet concrete, leaving its face exposed at the surface.
5
Fill the rest of the mold and smooth with a trowel.
6
While still wet, press your glass pieces into the surface in your chosen pattern, leaving the tops slightly exposed.
7
Smooth around the glass edges, then let the paver cure fully per your concrete product.
8
Demold and grind/sand the surface smooth to reveal the glass mosaic clearly. Repeat for all pavers.
9
Trench the path, lay landscape fabric, set the pavers, fill around them with white river rock, and edge both sides.
Styling & Finish Options
  • Vary the glass colors paver-to-paver so the path reads as a deliberate sequence, not a repeat pattern
  • Plant low border flowers right at the rock’s edge to soften the line
  • Add a couple of standalone solar path lights between pavers for extra evening ambience
Field Notes
  • Make one test paver first to dial in your concrete-to-glass ratio and grinding technique
  • Pre-cut glass mosaic sheets (craft/tile stores) are easier and safer than breaking your own glass
  • Stagger paver-making across a few days — each one needs cure time before it can be safely demolded

  • LED channels embedded in the resin mean the lighting is the floor, not an add-on

  • Scales from a single accent strip to a full room pour
What You’ll Need
Material / ItemQty & Notes
Whole bamboo poles, 3–4″ diameterEnough to span the room edge-to-edge, trimmed to length
Self-leveling 2-part epoxy resin (flooring grade)Sized to your sq ft per manufacturer’s coverage chart
Epoxy pigment / tint (optional)To taste
Warm-white LED strip, low-voltageLength = total channel run between bamboo rows
Mixing buckets + low-speed drill paddle2–3
Notched trowel / resin spreader1
Spiked degassing roller1
Painter’s tape + plastic sheeting (perimeter masking)As needed
Orbital sander + polishing pads1 set
Moisture-cure primer/sealer for the subfloorPer manufacturer spec
Low-speed mixing drill Notched trowel Degassing roller Heat gun (for bubbles) Orbital sander Shop vac VOC-rated respirator Nitrile gloves
Safety — Read Before Starting
Ventilation is mandatoryEpoxy resin off-gasses strong VOCs while curing. Run fans and keep the space ventilated for the full cure window, and wear a VOC-rated respirator while mixing and pouring.
Test your subfloor firstBamboo and resin both react to moisture. Confirm the subfloor is fully dry and sealed before laying poles, or trapped moisture can cloud or delaminate the cure.
Low-voltage lighting onlyOnly embed low-voltage LED strip rated for encapsulation. Never run mains-voltage wiring under a resin pour.
Mix in small batchesEpoxy generates heat as it cures. Large batches in one bucket can overheat and cure too fast — follow the manufacturer’s working time and batch-size limits exactly.
Cure fully before foot trafficSurface-cure and full-cure are different numbers. Stay off the floor entirely until the manufacturer’s full-cure time has passed, even if it feels solid sooner.
Step-by-Step
1
Prep the subfloor: clean, repair, and seal it so it’s fully flat, dry, and moisture-blocked.
2
Dry-lay the bamboo poles edge-to-edge across the room, trimming ends for a tight, consistent fit.
3
Secure the poles to the subfloor and run the LED strip channels in the gaps between selected rows.
4
Mask the perimeter and any fixtures with tape and plastic sheeting.
5
Mix the epoxy in small batches per the manufacturer’s pot-life window, tinting if desired.
6
Pour and spread the resin with a notched trowel, working from the far corner back toward the exit.
7
Pass a degassing roller over the wet surface, then hit any bubbles with a heat gun.
8
Let the floor cure undisturbed for the full manufacturer-rated cure time before any foot traffic.
9
Sand and polish the cured surface in stages to bring up the final gloss, then furnish the room.
Styling & Finish Options
  • Run the LED channels in a single direction for a clean, architectural line rather than a grid
  • A slightly amber-tinted resin warms the whole room at night
  • Pair with low, simple furniture so the floor stays the visual anchor
Field Notes
  • Do a small test pour in a closet or off-cut first to dial in your mix ratio and working time
  • Order 10–15% more bamboo than your measured run to allow for trimming and grain matching
  • Epoxy self-levels, but a notched trowel still helps move volume into low spots faster

  • A short section of leftover mesh makes a quick spark screen if you want one

Safety — Read Before Starting
Wet concrete is causticWet concrete can burn skin on contact. Wear gloves and avoid prolonged skin contact; rinse immediately if it touches bare skin.
Mind the dustDry concrete mix kicks up fine silica dust — wear a dust mask when pouring the dry bag and work in open air.
Leaf removal timing mattersPull the leaf too early and the texture smears; too late and it bonds in. Test on one paver to find your concrete’s right working window before doing the rest.

4
Let it set just enough to hold shape, then carefully peel the leaf away to reveal the imprint.
5
Let the paver cure fully per the concrete bag’s instructions.
6
Apply concrete stain to bring out the vein detail, then seal once dry.
7
Repeat for each paver, varying leaf size and angle so the path doesn’t look repetitive.
8
Lay the cured pavers into your path line, spacing them for a natural stride, and backfill the edges with soil or mulch.
Styling & Finish Options
  • Vary leaf species and paver size paver-to-paver so the path reads as organic, not stamped
  • A two-tone stain (darker veins, lighter body) makes the texture pop more than a flat color
  • Let grass grow right up to the paver edge instead of edging it in rock for a softer, garden look
Field Notes
  • Practice your leaf-press-and-peel timing on one test paver before committing to the full set
  • Big-box garden centers often carry oversized tropical leaves seasonally if you don’t have one on hand
  • Pour each paver directly on-site rather than in a mold — it’s what gives every one a slightly different shape

09Drum & Glass

Steel Drum
Whiskey Table

A reclaimed steel drum, cut down and fitted with a side opening, sanded back to bare metal, and painted satin black. A built-in wood shelf sits in the cutout for bottle storage, topped with a round glass cap and a hidden LED ring for a soft underglow.

Safety — Read Before Starting
Confirm the drum is empty and cleanNever cut into a drum that previously held fuel, chemicals, or solvent residue without fully degassing and cleaning it first — vapors in a sealed drum can ignite from a single spark.
Full PPE for grindingCutting and grinding steel throws sparks and metal dust. Wear eye protection, hearing protection, heavy gloves, and a long sleeve shirt.
Mind sharp cut edgesFreshly cut steel edges are razor sharp. File or grind every cut edge smooth before handling the piece bare-handed.
Tempered glass only for the topUse tempered or laminated glass for the top, never standard glass — it’s a surface for drinks and bottles and needs to be impact-safe.
Battery or low-voltage lighting onlyThe LED ring sits in a sealed-feeling cavity under the glass — never use mains-voltage wiring here.
Step-by-Step
1
Confirm the drum is fully empty, clean, and degassed before any cutting.
2
Mark your cut lines for height and the side cutout opening with a chalk marker.
3
Cut the drum down to height and cut out the side opening with an angle grinder.
4
Grind every cut edge smooth, then strip the surface back to bare metal with a wire wheel or flap disc.
5
Prime the bare metal with a rust-inhibiting primer, inside and out.
6
Paint with two coats of satin or matte black, letting each coat cure fully.
7
Cut and fit the wood shelf into the cutout opening, securing it level.
8
Lay the LED ring on the rim, then set the round glass top in place with felt bumpers or a thin silicone bead.
9
Style the open shelf with bottles or display pieces.
Styling & Finish Options
  • Keep the shelf styling simple — 3–4 bottles reads curated, a full shelf reads cluttered
  • A warm wood shelf against the black drum body gives the best material contrast
  • Works equally well as a planter stand if you skip the glass top
Field Notes
  • A flap disc cuts grinding time roughly in half versus a wire wheel alone on heavy rust
  • Have the glass shop cut the top slightly under the drum’s outer diameter so it doesn’t catch on the rolled rim
  • Test the LED ring’s brightness through the glass before you finalize placement — it dims more than you’d expect

Stacked Tire
Lounge Set

Safety — Read Before Starting
Source clean tiresAvoid tires with exposed steel belting or sidewall damage — stick to cosmetically worn but structurally sound take-offs.
Pilot-drill before screwing into sidewallTire sidewall is dense rubber over steel/fabric plies — pilot-drill each screw point to avoid snapping bits or stripping the screw.
Tempered glass only for the tableUse tempered or laminated glass for the top, never standard glass — it’s a surface for drinks and feet, and it needs to be impact-safe.
Stability check before useConfirm every stacked tire is fully screwed through to the one below before the chair or table holds weight.
Step-by-Step
1
Wash all tires thoroughly and let them dry completely.
2
For each chair, stack 2 tires for the base and pilot-drill + screw them together through the sidewalls.
3
Add a third tire angled back for the seat shape, securing it the same way.
4
Cut and fit wood boards into the seat and backrest openings, securing them to the tire sidewalls.
5
Stain or seal the wood pieces.
6
For the table, stack and screw 2–3 tires the same way as the chair base.
7
Style the top opening with terrarium fill, then set the round glass top in place.
8
Arrange the chairs around the table and add accent lighting if desired.
Styling & Finish Options
  • Keep all the wood tones matched across chairs and table for a cohesive set
  • A moss-and-miniature terrarium under glass reads far more intentional than open rock fill
  • String lights woven around the base tie the whole set together at night
Field Notes
  • Ask tire shops for matching tread tires so the whole set looks uniform
  • Pre-drill every hole — trying to screw directly into sidewall without a pilot hole is the #1 cause of stripped screws
  • Build one chair completely before starting the rest so you can dial in your screw pattern
